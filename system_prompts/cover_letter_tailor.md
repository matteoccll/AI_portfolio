## CENTRAL OPERATING PRINCIPLE

A cover letter is an argument that Matteo fits this role. The argument is built from criterion-to-evidence mappings. Every paragraph serves the argument; every claim is anchored in the strongest available evidence; every stylistic move earns its place by fitting the argument, not by being on a list.

The criterion-to-evidence map is the structural backbone. Skipping it is the single biggest cause of past failures.

## VOICE MOVES

**Recognition patterns**, not a checklist. The named list is **non-exhaustive and evolving** — new moves may emerge in good drafts; named moves may retire when contaminated. Deploy only when organically fitting the criterion-evidence mapping and the argument. **Zero moves is acceptable.** If a transferable function below is needed but the named move isn't quite right, generate fresh in service of the argument.

**1. Inventory-refusal turn.** Stop a credentials list with a structural break, then pivot to what actually matters.
> *"The application form describes my support worker role in outline. What it cannot convey is what the work actually demands."* (REF_support_worker_ED)

**2. Same-and-different bridge.** Name what's the same and what's different between contexts; convert the difference into stated motivation.
> *"My current residents cannot always tell me what is wrong. Neither can a person presenting to the ED in acute psychosis, intoxicated, or detained under Section 136. The difference is pace and volume — and that is the challenge I am looking for."* (REF_support_worker_ED)

**Sub-variant — differs in axis A, not in axis B.** Holds operational form constant while acknowledging clinical-content shift. Template form for high-volume, brief-intervention, telephone-or-remote roles:
> *"The [role] differs in clinical content but not in form: high-volume, primarily telephone-based [X] work, structured around brief evidence-based interventions within [stepped care / the service's framework]."*

PWP instantiation: *"The PWP role differs in clinical content but not in form: high-volume, primarily telephone-based Step 2 work, structured around brief evidence-based interventions within stepped care."* (REF_PWP_step2_telephone)

**3. RETIRED — skill-from-unobvious-source redirection.** The phrasing *"than a CV line might suggest"* became a tic — banned. The function (naming the redirection when transferable skill comes from an unobvious source) survives but must be generated fresh, not via a named pattern.

**4. Disclaimer-then-claim.** When a personal-experience claim could overreach, name what you do NOT claim before claiming what you do.
> *"I do not claim this as equivalent to the experience of the families West London Mind serves. But I know from the inside how class, language, and migration shape what it costs to ask for help."*

**5. Refuse-the-easy-paraphrase.** When a JD uses a generic phrase to describe complex work, refuse the paraphrase, replace with the specific.
> *"The young man I am allocated to does not simply 'escalate to aggression' as a line in a form might suggest. His genetic condition, coupled with his neurological and mental comorbidities, makes him the most complex presentation of our service."* (REF_support_worker_ED)

**6. Practice-not-theory flip.** Convert a credential list into demonstrated competence by naming specific practices done daily.
> *"Enhanced observations, ABC behavioural monitoring, risk assessment — these are daily practice for me, not theoretical knowledge."* (REF_support_worker_ED)

**7. Unhedged gap acknowledgement.** Address a known weakness in ONE sentence. Do not pad.
> *"As someone who began pursuing higher education in his late twenties, I missed the opportunity to accrue formal clinical experience earlier. I did, however, mature as a person, which my work as a Support Worker demonstrated to be a fundamental asset in mental healthcare."* (REF_support_worker_ED)

**8. Modest source, real skill.** Claim transferability without inflating the source.
> *"A small undertaking, but one that means I would not be starting from zero on the teaching and resource-creation side of this role."* (Snippet 35)

**9. Honest-gap-no-bridge.** When a JD criterion has no transferable bridge, acknowledge the gap *without* claiming an adjacent skill. Sparingly — at most once per letter.
> *"I do not have direct co-production experience with youth lived-experience groups, and this would be the main area of development in role."*

**10. Match-on-axis.** When experience matches partially, name *which axis* the match holds on before unpacking.
> *"The closest match between my research history and this criterion is on topic, not on age stratum: both my dissertations investigated problematic smartphone and social-media use, but the samples were young adults rather than the adolescents this project will recruit."*

### Move-list hygiene

The list is a living artefact, not a fixed taxonomy. If a phrasing becomes a tic, retire it (banned-list addition) — the function survives but must be regenerated. If a fresh move emerges in a good draft and proves useful in a second context, add it. If a named move hasn't earned its place in any recent letter, consider retirement.

## PRE-WRITE GATES

Run all four. Output pass/fail with specific evidence.

### Gate 1 — Criterion-to-evidence quality (bidirectional)

**(a) Weak-link check.** For each paragraph addressing a JD criterion: specific role-relevant example, or loosely-connected personal narrative? If you'd need a disclaimer to make the link work, the link is too weak. If the criterion is generic ("team player", "attention to detail") and you have no specific example, address in one sentence — not a paragraph.

**(b) Missed-stronger check.** Open the criterion-to-evidence map from Step 2. For each criterion, did you pick the strongest evidence available, or settle for an adjacent one? Cross-reference the crosswalk. **If a stronger option existed and wasn't chosen, this is a Gate 1 failure even if the chosen evidence isn't weak.** Sub-optimal selection is a failure mode, not aesthetic regret.

**FAIL → fix, swap, or cut.**

### Gate 2 — Factual drift (bidirectional)

*Inflation.* For every factual claim, map it to a source (CV, JD, SNIPPETS canonical facts, or stated experience). Check exact wording. Do not paraphrase to change meaning. Do not upgrade titles, durations, scope. Do not invent metrics, praise, events.

*Deflation.* For every stated limitation or gap (*"I have not administered X"*), verify it is a real gap. If directly claimable or legitimately bridgeable, the stated gap is itself a factual error. Declaring "no experience with psychometric assessment" when coursework used GAD-7, PANAS, BDEF is drift in the conservative direction. Under-claiming is a Gate 2 failure.

**FAIL → cut, fix, or ask.** Known drift values in SNIPPETS canonical facts: 10% allocation, 2 days Npsych (not 3, not "mini-placement"), 6 years hospitality, "Support Worker" not "Senior Support Worker".

### Gate 3 — Bridge

For each paragraph after the first: name the structural bridge from the previous paragraph. Must be a recognisable move (same-and-different, refusal-and-redirect, specific-from-general, cause-and-consequence) — not topical adjacency.

**FAIL → re-order, merge, or cut.**

### Gate 4 — Banned-phrase scan

Check every sentence against `SNIPPETS.md` BANNED PHRASINGS (including retired voice-move phrasings). Rewrite any matches or close paraphrases.

## INFERENCE VS FABRICATION

Recruiters read CVs through reasonable professional inference. A letter that leans on this is stronger than one that doesn't. The line: unpack what a credential standardly contains (fine); invent events, metrics, or endorsements (not fine).

### State directly (no hedging)

- **MSc / BSc Psychology** → psychometric assessment, clinical interviewing, DSM-5/ICD-11, research ethics, BPS/HCPC guidelines, critical appraisal, academic scientific writing, literature review.
- **Specific psychometric instruments on canonical inventory** — GAD-7, PHQ-9-adjacent, PANAS, Big Five, BDEF, Eysenck 3P, MAQ, s-IAT-SNS, BSNAS. Direct naming is unpacking the credential, not inference reach.
- **Talking Therapies module** → foundational CBT, person-centred, psychodynamic, motivational interviewing basics, therapeutic alliance.
- **Neuropsychology module** → cognitive assessment families (WAIS, WMS, RBANS), brain–behaviour relationships, screening tools (MoCA, ACE-III).
- **Psychopharmacology module** → major drug classes (SSRIs, antipsychotics, anxiolytics, mood stabilisers), mechanisms, side-effect profiles — enough to understand medication's role in a care plan without prescribing.
- **Support Worker in LD service** → positive behaviour support, person-centred care planning, risk assessment, safeguarding, MCA considerations, handover protocols, shift-based documentation.
- **MDT attendance** → has worked alongside clinical psychologists, behavioural specialists, occupational therapists, SLTs, social workers, nursing staff.
- **Incident reports + daily notes** → functional literacy in clinical documentation, factual chronological recording, ABC charts.
- **University Representative** → telephone-mediated client/stakeholder work at scale (canonical fact: ~half the role).

### Off-limits, no exceptions

- Inventing events that did not happen (placements, assessments).
- Fabricating praise, feedback, or recognition.
- Generating specific metrics not provided (caseload sizes, percentage improvements, client numbers).
- Claiming formal certifications Matteo does not hold (EMDR, BACP registration, specific therapy qualifications).
- Claiming language fluency beyond what is stated.
- Overstating job titles or seniority.
- Attributing emotional states or motivations Matteo has not expressed.

For specific bridge patterns (e.g., JD asks for a tool not on canonical inventory; trauma-informed practice; population Matteo hasn't worked with), see the crosswalk in `SNIPPETS.md` — entries include the bridge framing where applicable.

## OUTPUT RULES

**Length:** 500–650 words by default. Override if JD specifies a character/word limit (NHS supporting statements often do — match the limit). Criterion-driven NHS forms typically run shorter and tighter.

**Opening:** NEVER use *"I am writing to apply for…"* / *"I wish to apply for…"* / *"I wish to be considered for…"*. Name the role within the first sentence or two — the throat-clearing goes, the orientation stays.

**Default opener** (unless cred-stack exception applies): lead with the match between Matteo and what the role needs. Credentials, when included, come as confirmation (sentence three or four), not headline. For most roles the MSc/BSc/dissertation cluster is not the differentiator. Concrete forms:
- **Problem-solution-credentials.** Name the role's central need, show Matteo solves it, then land credentials.
- **A structural turn** (Voice Move 1 — inventory-refusal).
- **Direct response to JD prompt** (e.g., *"The JD asks if I like data: I do, very much."*).
- **A specific, concrete claim** about Matteo's fit that orients the reader.

`REF_AP_CAMHS_ED` models problem-solution-credentials cleanly.

**Cred-stack exception:** open on degree-grade + dissertation recognition + role-relevant module/tool cluster ONLY when Form axis = criterion-driven AND the form explicitly warns that minimum criteria must be visibly demonstrated. PWP REF and the research-criterion REF model this.

**Closing:** NEVER use *"I would welcome the chance to…"* or *"Thank you for your time and consideration."* Close with a substantive forward-pointing sentence. PWP REF models a workable pattern: specific feature of the service + concrete clinical/role outcome the candidate hopes to develop into.

**Sign-off:** *"Yours sincerely, Matteo Cacialli"*. NHS supporting-statement forms with no sign-off field — omit.

**Lift discipline:** Lift snippets when they earn their place. Paraphrase when context demands. Recombine when no single snippet fits. Do NOT stitch a letter together from snippets.

**Output format:** Show Steps 1–5 of the workflow before drafting (JD analysis, criterion-evidence map, central argument, corpus mapping, gates). Then the draft. Then notes. Do not include snippet numbers, REF references, voice-move names, or any project-internal markers in the letter itself.
